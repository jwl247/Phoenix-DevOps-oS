# Paint schemes for the remodel (pink-forward)

## How to use these schemes
Each scheme has three parts, using the 60-30-10 rule: 60% main wall color, 30% a supporting color (an accent wall, cabinets, big furniture, curtains), 10% a pop (pillows, art, hardware, a front door). Always test: paint two 12-inch sample squares on two different walls and look at them in the morning, afternoon and at night under your own bulbs before buying gallons.

## Scheme: Blush and sage (calm, fresh)
60% soft blush pink with a warm beige undertone on the walls. 30% muted sage green (cabinets, an accent wall, or bedding). 10% warm white trim plus brushed brass hardware. Pink and green sit across from each other on the color wheel, so they balance each other. Good for bedrooms, a craft room, a bathroom.

## Scheme: Dusty rose and charcoal (grown-up, cozy)
60% dusty rose (pink with a little gray in it) on the walls. 30% deep charcoal gray (an accent wall, a door, a sofa). 10% cream and matte black accents. The gray keeps the pink from feeling sweet. Good for a living room or an office.

## Scheme: Mauve and cream (soft, vintage)
60% warm cream walls. 30% mauve (a purple-leaning pink) on a feature wall, cabinets or furniture. 10% antique gold or wood tones. Good when you want pink without a pink room.

## Scheme: Coral pink and navy (bold, cheerful)
60% a light coral pink (pink leaning orange) on the walls. 30% navy blue (an accent wall, kitchen island, front door). 10% crisp white. Warm and cool together; lively for a kitchen, a kids' room or a sunroom.

## Scheme: Pale pink and white (bright, airy)
60% the palest pink, almost white, on walls and ceiling. 30% bright white trim, doors and cabinets. 10% one strong pink or raspberry in accessories. Makes small or dark rooms feel bigger.

## Undertones and light (why a pink looks different on your wall)
Every pink leans warm (toward peach, beige or orange) or cool (toward blue or purple). Warm pinks feel cozy; cool pinks feel crisp. North-facing rooms get cool, bluish light: pick a warmer pink there or it can look gray. South-facing rooms get warm light all day: cool pinks stay pink, warm pinks can turn peach. LED bulbs labeled 2700K are warm; 4000K and up are cool. Check samples under the bulbs you will actually use.

## Finishes (which sheen goes where)
Flat or matte: ceilings and low-traffic walls; hides flaws, harder to wash. Eggshell: most living spaces, a soft glow and wipeable. Satin: kitchens, bathrooms, kids' rooms, hallways; washable and handles humidity. Semi-gloss: trim, doors, cabinets; very durable and shows brush marks if rushed. High gloss: furniture and a statement front door.

## Pink accent wall tips
Choose the wall your eye lands on first when you walk in (behind the bed, behind the sofa, or the wall with the fireplace). Avoid a wall broken up by many windows or doors. Paint the accent wall last, after the room color is up, and tape the corner carefully.
