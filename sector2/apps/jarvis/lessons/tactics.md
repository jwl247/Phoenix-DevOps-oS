# Tactics (for the game, Sacrifice) - small-unit doctrine and real-time strategy

## Fire and maneuver
The core of small-unit tactics: one element fires to pin the enemy down (suppress) while another element moves to a better position, then they swap. Nobody moves in the open without someone covering them. Shoot, move, communicate.

## Cover versus concealment
Cover stops bullets (thick walls, earth berms, engine blocks, rocks). Concealment only hides you (bushes, tall grass, smoke, shadows, thin walls). Move from cover to cover; use concealment to stay unseen, but never mistake it for protection.

## Suppression
Suppressive fire makes the enemy keep their heads down so they can't shoot back accurately or move. It buys time for a flank, a retreat, or a casualty pull. In a game, suppression is a mechanic: units under fire lose accuracy and move slower.

## Flanking
Hit the enemy from the side or rear, where they have the least cover and the fewest guns facing. Fix them from the front with fire (pin them in place) while a second element maneuvers around. A flank that is seen coming becomes an ambush on the flankers, so move the flank through cover and keep the frontal fire going.

## Attacking a machine gun nest or bunker (assaulting a fortified position)
Never charge it head-on. Split the squad: a support-by-fire (base of fire) element sets up in cover where it can see the gun and suppresses it with steady fire, so the gunners keep their heads down. The assault element moves along a covered, concealed route to the side or rear of the position (the flank), ideally using smoke to block the gun's view while crossing open ground. When the assault element is close, the base of fire shifts or lifts its fire away from them so friends aren't hit, and the assault element takes the position from the flank. Then consolidate: secure the position, check for counterattack, treat casualties, redistribute ammunition. In a game: suppression mechanics, smoke, a flanking route and one squad holding fire on the gun are the pieces.

## Bounding overwatch
Moving under threat in two elements: one element stays in a covered position watching (overwatch) while the other bounds forward to the next covered position, then they swap. Slower than traveling, much safer when contact is likely. Successive bounds leapfrog; alternate bounds pass each other.

## Formations (fire team and squad)
Wedge: a V shape; good all-around control and firepower, the default in open terrain. File (column): one behind another; fast and easy to control in tight terrain, weak firepower to the front. Line: everyone abreast; maximum firepower forward, hard to control, weak flanks. Echelon: angled line; protects one flank. Pick the formation for the terrain and the threat direction.

## Combined arms
Use different arms so each covers the others' weaknesses: infantry protects tanks from close anti-tank teams, tanks protect infantry from machine guns and give armor, artillery and mortars suppress and break up enemy positions, recon finds targets, air defense protects everyone from aircraft. An enemy that counters one arm gets hit by another.

## Defense in depth
Don't put everything in one line. Layer the defense: a screen of scouts forward, the main defensive positions, and a reserve behind ready to counterattack. Make the enemy pay at each layer and keep a reserve uncommitted until you know where the real attack is.

## OODA loop
Observe, Orient, Decide, Act. The side that goes through this cycle faster forces the other to react to old information. Speed of decisions matters as much as firepower: scout first, decide quickly, act before the enemy adjusts.

## Ambush
Pick a kill zone the enemy must move through (a road, a bridge, a draw) with cover for you and none for them. Linear ambush: everyone along one side. L-shaped ambush: the long leg fires along the kill zone's length, the short leg fires across its front, giving crossing fire. Spring it all at once, then withdraw before the enemy can counterattack.

## Recon and intelligence
You can't fight what you can't see. Scout before committing forces: find enemy positions, strength and routes. Recon units avoid fights; they report. Deny the enemy information: kill their scouts, use concealment, fake movements.

## Terrain (key terrain, chokepoints, high ground)
Key terrain is any ground that gives a big advantage to whoever holds it (a hill overlooking a road, a bridge, a crossroads). High ground gives observation and fields of fire. Chokepoints (bridges, passes, narrow streets) funnel the enemy: defend them with few, attack through them with care and smoke. Use the acronym OAKOC: Observation and fields of fire, Avenues of approach, Key terrain, Obstacles, Cover and concealment.

## Logistics and supply lines
Armies run on fuel, ammunition, food and repairs. Protect your supply lines and cut the enemy's: a force without ammo or fuel is out of the fight. Forward supply points shorten the trip but are targets. Plan resupply before the battle, not during it.

## Urban operations
Cities favor the defender: buildings give cover, many angles and hidden firing positions. Attack building by building with overwatch, clear rooms with teams, avoid streets (kill zones), and move through walls or buildings when possible. Snipers and machine guns in upper floors dominate streets.

## Real-time strategy craft (scouting, map control, counters, macro and micro)
Scout early and often to see the enemy's build and plan. Map control: hold the spaces that give vision and resources, deny the enemy theirs. Counters: every unit has one it beats and one that beats it (rock-paper-scissors); build what counters what you scouted. Economy versus army: spend too much on the army and you fall behind later; too much on economy and you get rushed. Macro is the big picture (production, expansion, supply); micro is controlling individual units in a fight (focus fire, pulling back wounded units, kiting). Attack when you have an advantage in time, position or numbers, and retreat to preserve units rather than losing them all.
