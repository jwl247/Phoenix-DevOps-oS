# Color theory (the basics)

## The color wheel
Primary colors: red, yellow, blue. Secondary (mixing two primaries): orange, green, purple. Pink is a light (tinted) red; mauve is a pink-purple; coral is a pink-orange.

## Color combinations that work
Complementary: colors across the wheel, like pink and green, or coral and navy. High contrast and balanced. Analogous: neighbors on the wheel, like pink, coral and peach. Calm and harmonious. Monochromatic: several shades of one color, like blush, rose and raspberry. Elegant and simple. Neutral plus one color: cream, gray or white with one pink. Easy to live with.

## Warm and cool
Warm colors (reds, oranges, yellows, warm pinks) feel cozy and come forward. Cool colors (blues, greens, purples, cool pinks) feel calm and make a room feel bigger.

## Light and dark (value)
A tint is a color plus white (blush is a tint of red). A shade is a color plus black. A tone is a color plus gray (dusty rose is a toned pink). Light colors make small rooms feel bigger; dark colors make big rooms feel cozy.

## The 60-30-10 rule
60% main color (walls), 30% supporting color (furniture, an accent wall, cabinets), 10% accent (pillows, art, hardware). It keeps a room from feeling flat or busy.
