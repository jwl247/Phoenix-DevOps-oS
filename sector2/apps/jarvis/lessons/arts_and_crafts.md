# Arts and crafts

## Getting started (supplies worth having)
Acrylic paints (a basic set plus white and black), a few brushes (flat, round, detail), a cutting mat and craft knife, good scissors, a glue gun, Mod Podge (glue and sealer in one), cardstock, a ruler, pencils, and a storage box with dividers. Pink, white and metallic gold paints make almost any project look finished.

## Easy projects (an afternoon each)
Painted flower pots: clean terracotta pots, paint a base coat, add stripes, dots or florals, seal with clear spray. Decoupage boxes or trays: cut out napkins or paper, glue with Mod Podge, seal with two more coats. Handmade cards: fold cardstock, layer patterned paper, stamp or hand-letter a greeting. Painted rocks: wash and dry, paint a base, add designs with a dotting tool or paint pens, seal. Fabric-scrap garland: tie strips of fabric along a string; pink, white and floral scraps look great.

## Bigger projects (a weekend)
A seasonal door wreath: grapevine or foam ring, faux flowers, ribbon, hot glue. Macrame plant hanger: cotton cord and square knots (a beginner pattern needs about 40 feet of cord). Furniture makeover: clean, scuff-sand, prime, paint with chalk paint in a soft pink or sage, then seal with clear wax or a water-based topcoat. Framed gallery wall: paint thrift-store frames one color (gold or blush), fill with your own art or prints, lay them out on the floor first.

## Painting techniques for crafts
Dry brushing: almost no paint on the brush, swipe lightly for an aged look. Ombre: blend two colors (pink to white) while both are wet. Stenciling: hold the stencil flat, dab (don't brush) with a nearly dry sponge. Pour painting: mix acrylic with pouring medium, layer colors in a cup, flip onto the canvas, tilt to spread; wear gloves and protect the table.

## Craft room ideas (for the remodel)
Pegboard on one wall painted blush or sage, with hooks and small shelves for tools. Clear labeled bins so supplies are easy to find. A big table at standing or sitting height with a cutting mat on top. Good light: a bright daylight bulb (about 5000K) over the work table so colors look true. A rolling cart for current projects.

## Safety
Use a glue gun on a heat-proof mat and keep it away from little hands. Work with resin, spray sealers and spray paint only with good ventilation, and wear a mask and gloves for resin. Keep craft knives closed or capped. Read every label: some paints and glues aren't safe for food items.
