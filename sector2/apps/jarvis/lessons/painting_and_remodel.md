# Painting and remodel basics

## How much paint to buy
One gallon covers about 350 to 400 square feet with one coat on smooth, primed wall. Wall area = (all the wall lengths added up) times the ceiling height. Subtract about 20 square feet per door and 15 per window. Multiply by the number of coats (plan on 2). Divide by 350 and round up. Buy a quart, not a gallon, for small trim jobs. Use the paint_calc tool for exact numbers.

## Order of a room remodel
Demolition and repairs first. Then electrical and plumbing changes. Then drywall and patching. Then paint, top to bottom: ceiling first, then walls, then trim and doors. Flooring after painting (or protect new floors very well). Fixtures, outlets and switch covers go back on last.

## Prep (this is most of a good paint job)
Move furniture to the middle and cover it. Take off outlet and switch covers. Wash greasy walls (kitchens) with a mild degreaser. Fill holes with spackle, let it dry, sand smooth. Caulk gaps between trim and wall. Prime patches, stains, bare drywall, or when going from a dark color to a light one. Tape trim only if your hand isn't steady.

## Painting a wall
Cut in first: brush a 2 to 3 inch band along the ceiling, corners and trim. Then roll the open wall in a big W or M pattern, keeping a wet edge so roller marks don't show. Use a 3/8 inch roller nap for smooth walls and 1/2 inch for textured walls. Let the first coat dry fully (check the can, usually 2 to 4 hours) before the second.

## Safety
Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint: don't sand or scrape old paint without testing it first (test kits are sold at hardware stores). Keep windows open and a fan going. Use a sturdy ladder, never the top step. Wear eye protection when painting ceilings. Keep paint and solvents away from kids and pets. Never pour paint down a drain; ask your local waste program how to dispose of it.

## Tools to have
Angled 2 to 2.5 inch brush for cutting in, 9 inch roller with frame and extension pole, roller tray and liners, painter's tape, drop cloths, spackle and putty knife, sanding sponge, caulk and caulk gun, a stir stick, rags, and a step ladder.
